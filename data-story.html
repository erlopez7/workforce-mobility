The AI Threat

AI is already reshaping hiring. Young workers aged 22–25 are being hired 14% less into high-AI-exposure jobs since late 2022. Women are 16% more likely to work in high exposure occupations. Workers with degrees are often better positioned to use AI as a tool.

Model 1 — Job Automation Risk Predictor

🔵 Round 1 — Supervised Learning

Algorithm: Random Forest (100 decision trees)

Data: 25 manually labeled jobs

Training: Yes — learned from known examples (Frey & Osborne research)

X inputs (7): routine_cognitive_tasks, manual_tasks, social_tasks, nonroutine_cognitive_tasks, median_wage, employment_growth_pct, education_required_yrs

Y output: high_automation_risk (1 = high risk, 0 = safe)

Accuracy: 96% cross-validation | 80% test accuracy

Result: Cashier, Bookkeeper, Truck Driver as high risk; Nurse, Physical Therapist, Cybersecurity Analyst as low risk

Limitation: only worked for 25 jobs — not scalable

→ Updated in Round 2

🟢 Round 2 — Rule-Based Interpretable Scoring

Algorithm: Rule-based scoring system

Data: 1,016 occupations from full O*NET database

Training: No — uses economic theory + task structure logic

X inputs (5): routine_cognitive_tasks, nonroutine_cognitive_tasks, social_tasks, manual_tasks, job_zone

Y output: risk_score (0–4), risk_label (Low / Moderate / High)

Risk logic: +1 routine ≥ 0.40, +1 nonroutine ≤ 0.20, +1 social ≤ 0.10, +1 job_zone ≤ 2

Results: 446 Low Risk | 314 Moderate Risk | 256 High Risk

Validation: Matches real-world expectations for repetitive vs social/creative jobs

Why better: explainable and scalable to 1,000+ occupations

Model 2 — Youth Career Matching Engine

🔵 Round 1 — K-Means Clustering (5,000 tracts)

Algorithm: K-Means (unsupervised)

Data: 5,000 US census tracts

X inputs: pov20, mob_up, inc20, opp_gap

Y output: None — model discovers groups

Groups: Very Disadvantaged | Disadvantaged | Moderate Opportunity | Higher Opportunity

Scoring weights: Safety 35% | Job Growth 25% | Accessibility 25% | Income boost 15%

Verification: Very Disadvantaged mobility 0.291 | Higher Opportunity 0.626

→ Updated in Round 2

🟢 Round 2 — K-Means + KNN Imputation (73,202 tracts)

Algorithm: K-Means + KNN Imputation (k=5)

Data: Full 73,202 census tracts

New addition: KNN imputation filled 15.98% missing mobility values to 0% missing

X inputs: pov20, mob_up, inc20, opp_gap

Y output: None — unsupervised

Weights: Lower-opportunity groups Safety 45% + Accessibility 55%; Moderate/Higher Safety 60% + Accessibility 40%

Verification: 0.34 → 0.41 → 0.41 → 0.51 group ordering

Research Statement

How can we use public data to help low-income students identify educational pathways and maximize economic mobility? Our findings show that clearer pathways, lower-cost education options, and low-automation-risk jobs can improve long-term outcomes.
