Research Focus + AI Threat

Why this matters now: AI is already shifting entry-level hiring. Workers entering highly automatable roles face more risk, so clear pathways toward lower-risk, accessible careers are increasingly important.

Model 1 Used in Final Platform — Job Automation Risk

Method

Rule-based interpretable scoring on 1,016 O*NET occupations.

Inputs: routine tasks, nonroutine tasks, social tasks, manual tasks, job zone.

Output + Result

Risk score (0–4) and risk label (Low / Moderate / High).

Final split: 446 Low | 314 Moderate | 256 High.

Model 2 Used in Final Platform — Youth Opportunity Grouping + Career Match

Method

K-Means clustering on full tract dataset (73,202) with KNN imputation for missing mobility values.

Groups: Very Disadvantaged, Disadvantaged, Moderate Opportunity, Higher Opportunity.

Output + Result

Group-aware recommendation ranking with safety + accessibility weighting.

Group mobility ordering used in platform: 0.34 → 0.41 → 0.41 → 0.51.

Imputed mobility — missingness before vs after KNN

KMeans uses tract-level pov20, mob_up, inc20, and opp_gap. Raw merges left many tracts without upward mobility; KNN imputation fills those features so clustering and downstream UI logic do not drop rows or fail on nulls.

Key insight: Upward mobility missingness drops from about 16% to 0% after KNN imputation, allowing all 73,202 tracts to remain usable in the clustering pipeline. These values are estimated, not originally observed.

Youth opportunity clustering — group profiles

Mean tract features by youth_group from model2_cluster_profiles_mean.csv. Each axis is scaled 0–100 across the four groups (lowest group mean → 0, highest → 100) so poverty, mobility, income, and opportunity gap are comparable on one chart. Hover a point for raw values.

Note: Values are normalized to a 0–100 scale so income, poverty, mobility, and opportunity gap can be compared fairly in one group profile chart.
